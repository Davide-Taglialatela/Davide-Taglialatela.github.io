What is shown. Climate interpretation is the share of a county’s local disaster coverage that interprets the event as a manifestation of climate change, classified article by article with fine-tuned language models and averaged across the county’s outlets with circulation weights. Each year, counties are ranked and split into five equal groups; the map shows the group, not the underlying value. The “average” view ranks counties on their 2015–2024 mean. Grey counties have no local newspaper coverage in the sample.

Disaster episodes are the paper’s sample of floods, hurricanes, wildfires and coastal events, built from the NOAA Storm Events Database and retained when in the top 15 percent of an index of losses, fatalities and injuries. A county is marked as hit when NOAA records positive damage, deaths or injuries there for the episode. Episodes are dated by their start.

From What a Disaster Means: Attention, Interpretation, and the Demand for Green Assets. The county-year dataset underlying the map is not yet released; only quintiles are shown here.
