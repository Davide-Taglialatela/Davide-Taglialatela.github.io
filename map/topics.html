What disaster coverage talks about

Every disaster article in the corpus gets one category: skeptical of the climate link, climate-linked, episodic storytelling, human impacts, institutional response, disaster background and risk, or event facts only. Climate coverage in general

All articles that mention climate change, whatever the subject: what share is a disaster linked to climate change, climate policy, green energy and industry, climate change in general, or preparedness, disaster policy and insurance.

Categories. Skeptical: the article disputes the link between the disaster and climate change. Climate-linked: it connects the event to climate change, or has substantive sections on both. Episodic storytelling: it tells the event through individuals’ stories. Human impacts: victims and damage to homes are the central topic, but without storytelling. Institutional response: relief, agencies, insurance. Disaster background and risk: the article goes beyond the specific event, for example the hazard in general, how often it strikes, or what to expect in the future, without linking it to climate change. Event facts: only the specific event. Each article gets the first category that applies, in this order. An article with a climate link and background blocks is climate-linked, which keeps the background category small.

Corpus. 1,434,459 disaster articles published 2000–2024 by U.S. local and national outlets, classified paragraph by paragraph with the fine-tuned language models behind the climate interpretation measure. Shares are computed within outlet scope and year.

Climate coverage in general. An article enters when it has a paragraph on climate change, links a disaster to climate change, or is centred on climate policy. Classes, in order: disaster linked to climate change; climate policy; green energy and industry; preparedness, disaster policy and insurance; and climate change in general, the rest, mostly science, trends and projections.
