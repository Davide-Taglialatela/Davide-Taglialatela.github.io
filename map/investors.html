Local advisers. Circles mark counties with SEC-registered investment advisers whose ETF holdings are observed. Colour is the green share of those holdings (ETFs tracking green or climate indices): none, or the lower, middle and upper third of counties with any green ETFs in that year. Circle size is the third of counties by ETF holdings.

Mutual-fund managers. Triangles mark the counties where the managers of active U.S. equity mutual funds work (fund management offices, weighted when a team spans several counties). Colour is the county’s fifth by the funds’ portfolio tilt toward high environmental-score stocks (portfolio-weighted Refinitiv E-pillar score of the holdings, standardised within each year’s stock cross-section). Size is the number of funds managed there (1–2, 3–9, 10–39, 40 or more).

Listed firms. Diamonds mark the headquarters counties of U.S. listed firms with a Refinitiv environmental pillar score, 2015–2024. Size is the number of firms (1, 2–3, 4–15, 16 or more). Colour is the county’s fifth by the firms’ average environmental score or, when selected, by their average Trucost physical climate risk (mean sensitivity across cold waves, water stress, wildfire, drought, coastal and river floods, heat waves and hurricanes).

Disaster episodes are the paper’s sample of floods, hurricanes, wildfires and coastal events from the NOAA Storm Events Database, retained when in the top 15 percent of an index of losses, fatalities and injuries. A county is marked when NOAA records positive damage, deaths or injuries there. All 2015–2024 episodes are shown together.

Every quantity is shown as a group (thirds or fifths). From What a Disaster Means: Attention, Interpretation, and the Demand for Green Assets.
